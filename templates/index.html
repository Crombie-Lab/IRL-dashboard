Understanding Lagoon Water Levels and Flooding

The Indian River Lagoon is a shallow estuary with limited connections to the Atlantic Ocean. Water levels are influenced by several interacting factors, including wind, ocean tides, rainfall, and freshwater runoff from the surrounding watershed. Freshwater enters through creeks, canals, and stormwater systems, including engineered connections from portions of the neighboring Upper St. Johns River Basin. Elevated lagoon levels can also impede drainage from low-lying neighborhoods.

The relative importance of these factors for flooding near Briarwood Lane remains uncertain. By combining historical water-level observations with detailed rainfall, weather, and watershed monitoring data, we aim to identify the conditions driving high water levels and eventually develop and validate local flood forecasts.

The measurements presented here describe observed conditions, not road safety. Reported flood depths apply only to specific locations and observation times. Never drive through flooded roads.

Data and methods

Williams Point: Iowa Environmental Mesonet HML archive, station WIPF1 (NWS observations). Trident Pier: NOAA CO-OPS station 8721604 for actual ocean levels, tidal predictions, wind and pressure. Automatic-feed card timestamps use your browser's time zone; manual timestamps use Eastern time, both labeled explicitly. Chart axes use UTC. Lines break across gaps exceeding 30 minutes for water levels and 90 minutes for hourly products. Stale means an observation or site build is over 3 hours old; this is a freshness indicator, not a flood threshold. NOAA weather forecasts come from the NWS grid API; wind is converted from reported km/h to m/s, precipitation remains mm over its source period, and forecast update times over 12 hours old are flagged. Data are preliminary where their providers have not completed quality control.

Important: No automated flooding forecast, warning threshold, or road safety decision is provided. Check NWS Melbourne for official advisories. This is an independent informational project, not an official NOAA/NWS product.
