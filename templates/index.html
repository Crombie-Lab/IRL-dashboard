Why does high water persist along the lagoon?

The Indian River Lagoon is a shallow, elongated estuary with limited connections to the Atlantic Ocean. Sustained winds can redistribute lagoon water and elevate levels along some shorelines. High lagoon water can also reduce the hydraulic gradient for stormwater outfalls, slowing drainage from low-lying streets and neighborhoods. Local rainfall, ground elevation, blocked or undersized drains, and tidal conditions can also contribute.

The gauge and weather plots describe regional conditions; manual markers report water depth only at the sampled road points and times. They do not establish whether any street is passable. Do not drive through flooded roads.

Data and methods

Williams Point: Iowa Environmental Mesonet HML archive, station WIPF1 (NWS observations). Trident Pier: NOAA CO-OPS station 8721604 for actual ocean levels, tidal predictions, wind and pressure. Card timestamps use your browser's time zone (shown explicitly); chart axes use UTC. Lines break across gaps exceeding 30 minutes for water levels and 90 minutes for hourly products. Stale means an observation or site build is over 3 hours old; this is a freshness indicator, not a flood threshold. NOAA weather forecasts come from the NWS grid API; wind is converted from reported km/h to m/s, precipitation remains mm over its source period, and forecast update times over 12 hours old are flagged. Data are preliminary where their providers have not completed quality control.

Important: No automated flooding forecast, warning threshold, or road safety decision is provided. Check NWS Melbourne for official advisories. This is an independent informational project, not an official NOAA/NWS product.
