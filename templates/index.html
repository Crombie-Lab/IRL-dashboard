About the level colors

The colors show exploratory ranges from five north-culvert readings on November 5–7, 2026. The fitted line reaches zero road-center depth at 1.744 ft MHHW; its nominal 95% interval is 1.652–1.810 ft. Yellow uses only the lower part of that interval. Orange ends 3 inches of lagoon-level rise above 1.744 ft, at 1.994 ft.

These are preliminary comparison bands from one falling-water episode, not verified drying levels, current road depths, or road-safety ratings. The green band does not establish that the road is dry.

Wind symbols & rain totals

Wind symbols point toward where the wind comes from. A half feather means 5 knots (about 6 mph), a full feather 10 knots (12 mph), and a triangle 50 knots (58 mph). Circles show speeds rounded below 2.5 knots (3 mph).

Rain points are totals for each NOAA period, usually 6 hours, including any part before now. Lines between points are visual guides, not hourly rain estimates. Tap or hover for the period and amount.

The rain card totals adjoining periods with rain forecast. Dry or missing periods break the group. Times are forecast windows, not exact start/stop times. Peak chance is the highest NOAA period probability, not a combined event probability.

Why lagoon water rises

Wind, ocean tides, rain, and runoff all affect lagoon levels. Creeks, canals, and storm drains bring in freshwater, including engineered links from parts of the Upper St. Johns River Basin. High lagoon water can slow neighborhood drainage.

We don’t yet know which factors matter most near Briarwood Lane. We’re comparing water levels, rain, and weather to work toward tested local flood forecasts.

Road depths apply only where and when measured. Avoid driving through flood waters if possible.

Sources & measurement details

Williams Point: IEM / NWS WIPF1, feet relative to MHHW. Trident Pier: NOAA 8721604, ocean levels and astronomical tides in feet relative to MLLW; wind in mph and pressure in hPa. MHHW and MLLW are different reference levels. Neither is road depth, and heights at different stations cannot be directly subtracted.

All displayed times use local Eastern time (EST in winter, EDT in summer). Data gaps stay blank. Automatic readings and site updates over 3 hours old are flagged; NOAA forecast updates are flagged after 12 hours. These are freshness checks, not flood thresholds.

Road-depth dots use a separate inches scale. Their position against the lagoon line does not establish a relationship. Manual readings are user-reported; the first north-culvert reading was at a nearby spot. NOAA forecasts describe future weather, not lagoon flooding. Forecast rain keeps NOAA’s original periods; a zero amount can accompany a nonzero rain chance.

Observations may be preliminary. Updates are attempted hourly and can be delayed.
